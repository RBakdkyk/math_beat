## Topic א — השבר הפשוט (Simple Fractions) — 25 hours

### Background
In 3rd grade students worked with basic fractions and eighths. In 4th grade they encounter a much wider range of fractions and deepen understanding of fraction meaning.

### Subtopic א.1 — הכרת השבר הפשוט (Understanding Simple Fractions) — 10 hours

#### Concepts and Terminology
- **מונה** (numerator), **מכנה** (denominator), **קו שבר** (fraction bar), **מספר מעורב** (mixed number)
- Fraction as **part of a whole** (חלק מהשלם)
- Fraction as **part of a quantity** (חלק מכמות)
- Fraction as **ratio** (יחס) — introduced here
- Fraction as **quotient** (מנה) — may be introduced here, especially in advanced classes

#### Models Used (Concrete Representations)
Students must build understanding using multiple physical/visual models:
- Circles (פרוסות עוגה — pie slices)
- Rectangles (מלבנים)
- Groups/sets (קבוצות)
- Number line (ישר המספרים)

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Draw a given fraction of a rectangle (e.g., draw ⅔ of a given rectangle)
- Find a fraction of a quantity using drawings (e.g., find 5/10 of pie slices using pictures)
- Given a drawn fraction, reconstruct the whole
- Determine how many circles can be covered by a given number of fraction pieces
- Recognize that fractions can have **different names** (שמות שונים לשבר) — equivalent fractions
- Convert between improper fractions and mixed numbers
- Represent 0 and any natural number as a fraction (e.g., 0/5 = 0, 6/2 = 6/1 = 6)
- Sort fractions into three groups: less than 1, equal to 1, greater than 1

#### Equivalent Fractions (שמות שונים)
- Equivalent fractions are discovered through **concrete manipulation** (e.g., placing two quarter-squares on a half-square)
- This is **intuitive preparation** for reducing/expanding fractions, which is formally taught in 5th grade
- The numerator can be greater than the denominator
- Example: 1/4 = 2/8 = 3/6; 3/2 = 1½

#### Fraction Comparison (השוואת שברים)
- Method: **intuitive, without algorithm** (ללא אלגוריתם)
- Strategies taught:
  - Same numerator comparison (e.g., 1/7 vs 1/9 — which is larger?)
  - Same denominator comparison
  - Proximity to ½ or 1 (e.g., which is closer to 1: fractions like 3/4 vs 4/5 vs 5/6?)
  - Which fractions are greater than ½?

#### Example Question Types — Fractions Understanding
- "Draw ⅔ of the given rectangle"
- "Find 5/10 of the pie — use drawings"
- "The fraction 1/5 is drawn [of the whole] — draw the whole"
- "How many circles of size 1 can be covered by 6 pieces of size 1/3?"
- "Which is larger: 1/7 or 1/9?" (same numerator — intuitively larger denominator means smaller piece)
- "Which is closer to 1: [fractions presented]?"
- "From the fractions 2/5, 3/5, 4/8, 10/8, 8/4, 5/8 — find pairs with different names for the same fraction"
- "Find fractions greater than ½ from: 1/2, 1/4, 3/5, 1/3"
- Sort fractions as greater than 1, equal to 1, or less than 1
- Given 6/2, 5/4, 8/8, 0/23, 4/8 etc. — classify them

### Subtopic א.2 — פעולות בשברים — היכרות ראשונה (Fraction Operations — First Introduction) — 15 hours

#### Addition and Subtraction with Equal or Related Denominators

**Rule**: Do NOT rely on formal algorithms for reducing/expanding. Work only through:
1. Concrete objects/models
2. Previously learned knowledge of equivalent fraction names

**Equal denominators**: Direct addition/subtraction
- Example: 4/5 − 2/5; 1/3 + 2/3

**Related denominators** (one denominator is a multiple of the other):
- Students solve exercises where one denominator is a multiple of the other
- The relationship must use **familiar numbers**
- Example: 3/6 and 1/3; 5/6 and 1/6 (where 6 is a multiple of 3)
- Example: 2/3 + 1/6 = ? → first recognize that 2/3 = 4/6, then add

**Missing number problems**:
- □ − 1/4 = 3/4
- 1 + □ = 1½
- The exercise 1/2 + □ can be solved by recognizing 1/2 as 2/4

**Addition and subtraction of mixed numbers** with equal or related denominators

#### Multiplication of a Whole Number by a Fraction
- Taught as **repeated addition** (כפל שלם בשבר יילמד כחיבור חוזר של אותו השבר)
- Example: 3 × 1/5 = 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5

#### Subtopic א.3 — שאלות מילוליות בשברים (Word Problems with Fractions)
- Students must identify the **unit** (יחידה) and the **part** (חלק) in word problems
- Problem types connected to the meaning of a fraction

#### Example Question Types — Fraction Operations
- "3/5 + 1/5 = ?" (equal denominators, addition)
- "2 + 3/4 = ?" (whole plus fraction)
- "3/3 − 1/4 = ?" (related denominators)
- "1 − 1/3 = ?" (subtract from whole)
- "□ − 1/4 = 3/4" (missing number)
- "1 + □ = 1½" (missing addend)
- "2/3 + 1/6 = ?" (one denominator is multiple of other)
- "3 × 1/5 = ?" (whole × fraction as repeated addition)
- Word problem: "Rena got ½ of a cake, Tamar got ¼ of the same cake. Who got more? What fraction did they get together? What fraction is left?"
- Word problem: "Together Michal and Dina got ½ of a cake. Find three possibilities for how it was divided between them"
- Word problem: "Yossi lost the 1/3 slice from his circle. How can he reconstruct it from slices of other fractions?"
- Word problem: "30 students in class, 1/3 participate in basketball. How many students?"

#### Pedagogical Constraints — Fractions
- Fraction comparison must be **intuitive** — no cross-multiplication or common denominator algorithms in grade 4
- Equivalent fractions taught through **physical manipulation**, not rules
- Formal reduction/expansion of fractions (צמצום והרחבה) is a **5th grade topic**
- Mixed number addition: only with equal or related (multiple) denominators
- Denominator values should be small and familiar to students (halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths, eighths, tenths)

## Topic ב — המספרים הטבעיים עד מיליון (Natural Numbers up to One Million) — 10 hours

### Subtopics

#### ב.1 — עקרונות המבנה העשרוני (Decimal System Structure) — Review
- Revision of place value principles from earlier grades
- Extension to the **millions** domain

#### ב.2 — קריאה וכתיבה של מספרים (Reading and Writing Numbers)
- Reading and writing numbers up to 1,000,000
- Standard formatting: digits grouped in threes with commas or spaces (e.g., 1,257,324)
- Understanding there is no largest number (no upper bound)
- Students write 3 numbers larger than one million, then write a number larger than each

#### ב.3 — שיטת האלף-בית / גימטרייה (Hebrew Letter Numeral System / Gematria)
- Knowing the numeric value of each Hebrew letter
- Writing numbers using the Hebrew aleph-bet system
- Finding the numeric value of different words
- Calendar calculations in the Hebrew year system

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Read and write natural numbers up to 1,000,000
- Understand that any number can be written with 3-digit groupings
- Work with the decimal place-value structure
- Know Hebrew letter values and perform gematria calculations
- Appreciate that numbers have no upper bound

#### Example Question Types — Natural Numbers
- "Estimate how many words or printed characters are in a book of 100 pages (approximately)"
- "Write three numbers larger than one million. Can you write a number larger than each of them?"
- "Write the number 1,257,324 in groups of three"
- Gematria: "Find the numeric value of the word [Hebrew word]"
- "How many days without sleep would it take to count to one million (counting one number per second)?"

#### Pedagogical Notes
- Illustrate one million visually: a square of millimeter graph paper with sides of one meter contains approximately one million squares
- Emphasize the infinite nature of natural numbers

- Review from prior years, extended to larger number domains

- This topic is review/consolidation — students already know the number line from earlier grades

## Topic ד — פעולות החשבון (Arithmetic Operations) — 50 hours

### Subtopic ד.1 — חיבור וחיסור ללא הגבלה (Addition and Subtraction — Unlimited)

#### Scope and Constraints
- **No upper limit** on number size (ללא הגבלה)
- Multi-digit addition and subtraction in **column format** (במאונך)
- Mental arithmetic: addition of two addends
- Written: three or more addends

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Add and subtract multi-digit numbers in column format
- Perform mental addition with two addends
- Detect errors in column addition (e.g., alignment errors — shown with example of Amir's incorrect solution)
- Solve mental exercises like: 1,200 + __ = 1,314
- Find three 3-digit numbers whose sum exceeds 2,000
- Compare expressions without computing (e.g., 2,753 − 285 __ 2,753 − 392 using > or <)

#### Example Question Types — Addition and Subtraction
- Column addition/subtraction of 4-5 digit numbers
- "Amir wrote 433 + 1,501 in column format and got 5,831. Is he right? Explain." (alignment error)
- Mental: "1,200 + __ = 1,314"
- "Find three 3-digit numbers whose sum is greater than 2,000"
- "Write > < or = without computing: 2,753−285 __ 2,753−392"
- Find missing digits in a column addition problem

---

### Subtopic ד.2 — כפל במאונך בגורם רב-ספרתי (Multiplication in Column with Multi-Digit Factor)

#### Foundation
- Based on the **decimal place-value structure** and the **distributive law** (חוק הפילוג)

#### Mental Multiplication
- Multiplication by whole tens and whole hundreds (כפל בעל פה בעשרות שלמות ובמאות שלמות)

#### Written Multiplication
- 2-digit × 2-digit (in column format)
- 3-digit factor multiplication for capable students

#### Methods Taught
1. **Expanded form using distributive law**:
   - 27 × 35 = 20×35 + 7×35 = 20×35 + 7×30 + 7×5 = 700 + 210 + 35 = 945
2. **Table/grid method** (partial products grid):
   - Row/column headers for tens and units, filling in all four partial products
3. **Standard algorithm** (האלגוריתם המקובל)

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Mentally multiply by whole tens and hundreds
- Multiply 2-digit × 2-digit using multiple methods
- Build a problem so that a specific product property holds (e.g., "use digits 1-4 each once to build two numbers with the largest possible product")
- Understand why multiplication works through the distributive law

#### Example Question Types — Multiplication
- Mental: "25 × 30 = ?", "400 × 20 = ?"
- "Calculate 27 × 35 using the distributive law"
- "Calculate 27 × 35 using the grid method"
- "Calculate 27 × 35 using the standard algorithm"
- "Using digits 1, 2, 3, 4 (each once), build two numbers so their product is as large as possible"
- Estimation: "Is 35 × 42 > 1,000?" (with justification)

### Subtopic ד.3 — חילוק ארוך (Long Division)

#### Constraints
- **Divisor**: single-digit (חד-ספרתי) **or** whole tens (עשרת שלמה)
- Both **with and without remainder** (עם שארית וללא שארית)
- Important to practice **mental division** before using the algorithm
- Division domain in competency standards: **up to 100** (with or without remainder)

#### Methods Taught
1. **Repeated subtraction method** (דרך החיסור החוזר):
   - Guess a partial quotient, subtract its product from the dividend, repeat until remainder < divisor
   - Example: 524 ÷ 4 → guess 50 (50×4=200), remainder 324; guess 65 (65×4=260), remainder 64; guess 11 (11×4=44), remainder 20; guess 5 (5×4=20), done. Quotient = 50+65+11+5 = 131
   - Based on the distributive law
2. **Standard long division algorithm** (האלגוריתם המקובל):
   - Based on decimal structure and distributive law

#### Pedagogical Language Note (Critical)
- The curriculum explicitly prescribes correct mathematical language:
  - CORRECT: "4 divides into 500 once (one time in five hundreds)" — i.e., use place-value language
  - INCORRECT: "4 goes into 5 once" — this strips place value meaning
  - In 427÷5: prefer "4 cannot be divided by 5" over "5 goes into 4 zero times"
  - Correct: "4 divided by 5 equals zero (with remainder 4)"

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Perform mental division (basic facts)
- Solve long division with single-digit divisor, with and without remainder
- Solve long division with a whole-tens divisor
- Execute both the repeated-subtraction method and the standard algorithm
- Check division using multiplication

#### Example Question Types — Long Division
- Mental: "81 ÷ 3 = ?"
- "524 ÷ 4 using repeated subtraction"
- "527 ÷ 4 using the standard algorithm"
- Division with whole-tens divisor: "840 ÷ 20 = ?"
- Problems with remainder: "What is 85 ÷ 6? What is the remainder?"
- Multi-step: "843 students returned from theatre by bus, 51 seats per bus. How many buses were needed? How many empty seats?"

### Subtopic ד.4 — אומדן ופיתוח תובנה מספרית (Estimation and Number Sense)

#### Purpose
- Estimation is important both in its own right and as a tool for developing number sense and problem-solving strategies

#### Estimation Techniques
- Round numbers to the nearest ten or hundred, then operate
- Example: 525 × 18 ≈ 500 × 20 = 10,000
- Alternative strategies allowed: e.g., 35 × 42 = 70 × 21 > 70 × 20 = 1,400

#### Contexts for Estimation
1. Estimating before computing (to check feasibility)
2. Checking reasonableness of a computed result
3. Real-life situations where approximation is sufficient
4. Situations requiring estimation as the only practical approach

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Estimate results of all four operations
- Judge whether a computed answer is reasonable
- Distinguish between approximate numbers and exact numbers in context
- Estimate real-world quantities (words per minute, grains of rice per kilogram, thickness of a page)

#### Example Question Types — Estimation
- "Is 35 × 42 greater than 1,000? Show your reasoning without computing exactly."
- "For 18 × 47, the result was 126. Is this reasonable?"
- "A class of 37 students goes on a trip costing 98 shekels each. Approximately how much was collected?"
- "How many words can you write in an hour? In a minute?"
- "Approximately how many grains of rice are in a 1kg bag?"
- "How can you find the thickness of one page in a book?"
- Game "4 in a Row" (4 בקו ישר): estimate products of two numbers from a list, cover the matching cell in a grid — first to cover 4 in a row wins

#### Number Sense Activities
- "Given 81 ÷ 5, by how much must you increase the dividend to get a division without remainder? Are there other answers?"
- Division using distribution: 51÷3 = (30+21)÷3 = 10+7 = 17
- "Complete: 18+42 = 19+__"
- "Given 14×5=70 → 14×50 = __"
- "Given 15+15+15+15=60 → 60÷15 = __"
- "If the dividend doubles, how does the quotient change?"
- "Is the result even or odd? (without computing): 235+387, 387×235, 387−235, 387−236"
- "Which is greater: 327÷186 or 327÷186+1?" and "By how much?"
- Building addition/subtraction/multiplication/division to approximate 300 using digits 1,2,3,4 each once

### Subtopic ד.5 — שאלות כוללות ואינטגרטיביות (Comprehensive and Integrative Word Problems) — 12 hours

#### Problem Types
- **Single-step problems**: addition, subtraction, multiplication, division with "large" natural numbers
- **Two-step problems**: can be solved as two separate exercises or as one compound expression with brackets

#### Two-Step Problem Structures
The curriculum explicitly enumerates these forms:
- a ± b : c
- a × b × c
- (a ± b) × c
- a × b : c
- (a ± b) : c

#### Pedagogical Note
- The teacher should present the second method (compound expression) but must not force all students to use only one approach
- Students may compose their own problems from given data

#### Example Word Problems
- "A work manager pays his workers 15,000 shekels/month. Each worker earns 2,500 shekels. How many workers does he employ?" → 15,000 ÷ 2,500
- "A theatre hall has 1,700 seats. 843 students from school Yovelim and 611 from school Negohot, plus 129 teachers were invited. Is there room for everyone?"
- "300 shekels were collected for a Purim party. They bought 35 gifts at 6 shekels each. How much money is left for other expenses?" → a − (b × c)
- "270 orange seedlings at a nursery. Planters bought equal amounts; the seventh planter bought 90 seedlings. How many did each of the other 6 buy?" → (a − b) ÷ c
- "843 students returned by bus, 51 seats per bus. How many buses? How many empty seats?"
- Problems involving measurements (length, weight)

### Subtopic ד.6 — סדר הפעולות והשימוש בסוגריים (Order of Operations and Use of Brackets)

#### Background
- Order of operations was taught in 3rd grade
- In 4th grade: **review and practice with larger numbers**

#### Rules Covered
- Standard order: brackets first, then multiplication/division, then addition/subtraction
- Use of brackets to change order

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Apply order of operations correctly
- Evaluate expressions with brackets
- Predict whether different arrangements of brackets/operations produce the same result

#### Example Question Types — Order of Operations
- "47 × 5 − 63 ÷ 3 = ?"
- "5,200 × 100 ÷ 10 = ?"
- "7,225 − (4,894 + 27) = ?"
- "Estimate — do all these give the same result? (then compute to check):
  - (7+3) × 10 ÷ 2
  - 7 + 3 × (10÷2)
  - (7+3) × (10÷2)
  - 7 + 3 × 10 ÷ 2"

### Subtopic ד.7 — תכונות ה-0 וה-10 (Properties of 0 and 1)

#### Background
- Taught in 3rd grade; reviewed in 4th grade

#### Properties of 0
- a + 0 = a (additive identity)
- a − 0 = a
- a × 0 = 0 (multiplication by zero)
- 0 × a = 0
- a ÷ 0 is **undefined** (חסר משמעות) — no number times 0 equals a non-zero number
- 0 ÷ 0 is **undefined** — every number times 0 is 0, so there is no unique answer
- 0 ÷ a = 0 for a ≠ 0

#### Properties of 1
- 1 × a = a × 1 = a (multiplicative identity)
- a ÷ 1 = a
- a ÷ a = 1 for a ≠ 0

#### Pedagogical Note
- Students should be able to articulate these properties in words (according to ability)
- Advanced classes may express properties as formulas
- Students compare properties of 0 and 1 in addition vs. multiplication

### Subtopic ד.8 — מספרים ראשוניים ופריקים (Prime and Composite Numbers)

#### Definitions
- **מספר ראשוני** (prime): a natural number with exactly two distinct factors (1 and itself)
- **מספר פריק** (composite): a number with more than two factors
- **1 is neither prime nor composite** — this is explicitly noted

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Distinguish prime numbers from composite numbers
- Factor numbers up to **100** into prime factors
- Build a number from its prime factors
- Know that every composite number has a **unique prime factorization** (Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic — introduced intuitively)

#### Example Question Types — Prime/Composite
- "Is 12 prime or composite?" → 12 = 2 × 2 × 3
- "Is 101 prime or composite? Check."
- "Factor into prime factors: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3 = 2 × 3 × 2" (order doesn't matter, result is same)
- Identifying primes up to 100

### Subtopic ד.9 — סימני התחלקות (Divisibility Rules)

#### Rules Covered
- Divisibility by **3**: sum of digits divisible by 3
- Divisibility by **6**: divisible by both 2 and 3
- Divisibility by **9**: sum of digits divisible by 9

#### How They Are Taught
- Students **discover** divisibility rules through investigating the multiplication table and digit sums — not memorized by fiat
- Justification using decimal structure: 9, 99, 999, etc. are all divisible by 9 (and by 3), so each positional value leaves only the digit itself as remainder

#### Example: Justification for Rule of 9
- 10 = 9 + 1 → dividing 10 by 9 leaves remainder 1
- 100 = 99 + 1 → same
- Therefore any number's remainder when divided by 9 equals the remainder of its digit sum divided by 9

#### Note on Divisibility by 6
- A number is divisible by 6 if it is divisible by both 2 and 3
- Discussion: if a number is divisible by 2 and by 4, is it divisible by 8? (No — illustrate with counterexample, explain using prime factorization)

### Subtopic ד.10 — חזקות (Powers/Exponents)

#### Scope
- Exponents introduced as **shorthand notation for repeated multiplication** (ככתיב מקוצר לכפל חוזר)
- Analogy made explicit: multiplication is repeated addition; exponents are repeated multiplication

#### Key Points
- Power is NOT a new binary operation — it is not commutative
- 2^5 ≠ 5^2 (explicit example given in the document)
- Students learn the meaning of base and exponent
- Special cases: any base to the power 1 equals the base; any base to the power 0 equals 1 (introduced)

#### Example Question Types — Powers
- "What is 2^3?" (= 8)
- "Is 2^5 equal to 5^2?" (no: 32 ≠ 25)
- "Write 8 as a power of 2"

## Topic ה — חקר הנתונים וניתוח סיכויים (Data Analysis and Probability) — 8 hours

### Subtopic ה.1 — חקר נתונים (Data Investigation) — 4 hours

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Collect, organize, and represent data in various ways
- Read and interpret diagrams from newspapers
- Build data projects with classmates (e.g., from geography or social studies)
- Describe phenomena from different domains using diagrams

#### Representations Covered
- Bar charts (דיאגרמת עמודות)
- Horizontal bar charts (דיאגרמת עמודות מוטות)
- Double or triple bar charts for comparing sets (דיאגרמת עמודות כפולה/משולשת)
- Various graphical representations

#### Example Activities
- Count types of vehicles passing the school at different hours
- Chart rainfall amounts in different regions over different years
- Compare data from multiple years using double bar charts
- Read and discuss diagrams from newspapers

### Subtopic ה.2 — ניתוח סיכויים (Probability Analysis) — 4 hours

#### Terminology
- **אפשרי** (possible / "might happen")
- **בלתי אפשרי** (impossible)
- **ודאי / בטוח** (certain / "definitely")

#### Skills: What the Child Must Do
- Identify situations of certainty vs. uncertainty vs. impossibility
- Compare likelihoods of events
- Propose their own examples of certain, impossible, and possible events

#### Example Question Types — Probability
- "Write 'possible', 'impossible', or 'certain':
  - Rain/snow on the way home from school
  - Meeting a specific animal on the way home
  - Going to school tomorrow
  - Drawing a chocolate candy from a sealed bag (colors: red, blue, black, pink, ...)"
- "What is more likely when rolling a die:
  - Even number
  - Number divisible by 4
  - Number divisible by 7
  - 7"
- "What is more likely — a student absent tomorrow has a name starting with א or starting with ק?"

## Topic ו — צורות וגופים (Shapes and Solids) — 15 hours

### Subtopic ו.1 — מצולעים, אלכסון (Polygons, Diagonal) — 3 hours

#### Concepts
- Review: polygon (מצולע), vertex/corner (קדקוד), side (צלע)
- **Diagonal** (אלכסון): a line segment connecting two non-adjacent vertices (vertices not on the same side)
- Note: a diagonal can be inside the polygon, outside it, or partially on a side

#### Skills
- Identify and draw diagonals in polygons
- Count diagonals in various polygons

---

### Subtopic ו.2 — ריבוע ומלבן (Square and Rectangle) — 4 hours

#### Concepts
- Formal definitions of square and rectangle
- Properties investigated: parallel sides, equal sides, equal angles
- Key relationship: **a square is a special rectangle** (הריבוע הוא מלבן מיוחד)

#### Skills
- Define square and rectangle
- Identify properties: side equality, angle equality, side parallelism
- Draw a right isosceles triangle (משולש ישר זווית שווה שוקיים)
- Determine: "Can an obtuse triangle be isosceles?"

---

### Subtopic ו.3 — תכונות של צלעות וזוויות במשולש (Properties of Sides and Angles in a Triangle) — 2 hours

#### Triangle Types Covered
- **משולש חד זוויות** — acute triangle (all angles acute)
- **משולש ישר זווית** — right triangle (one 90° angle)
- **משולש קהה זווית** — obtuse triangle (one obtuse angle)
- **משולש שווה שוקיים** — isosceles triangle (two equal sides)
- **משולש שווה צלעות** — equilateral triangle (all sides equal) — implied
- **משולש שונה צלעות** — scalene triangle (all sides different)
- **דלתון** — kite / deltoid

#### Skills
- Classify triangles by angles and by sides
- Explore symmetry properties of triangles (see Topic ז)

---

### Subtopic ו.4 — תיבות (Rectangular Prisms / Cuboids) — 6 hours

#### Terminology
- **תיבה** (rectangular prism / cuboid)
- **פאה** (face)
- **צלע / מקצוע** (edge)
- **קדקוד** (vertex/corner)
- **קובייה** (cube — a special cuboid)
- **אלכסון פאה** (face diagonal)
- **אלכסון תיבה** (space diagonal — from one corner to the opposite corner)

#### Key Concepts
- A cuboid has all rectangular faces (a square is a special rectangle, so a cube is a special cuboid)
- **Space diagonal** (אלכסון מרחבי): longer than any other segment contained in the cuboid
- **Net** (פריסה): unfolding a cuboid into a flat shape
  - Multiple nets of the same cuboid are possible
  - Some arrangements of rectangles are NOT nets (do not fold into a cuboid)

#### Skills
- Identify faces, edges, vertices of a cuboid
- Determine whether a set of rectangles can form a cuboid
- Build a cuboid from real cubes
- Match a net to a cuboid and vice versa
- Distinguish face diagonal from space diagonal
- Identify which among given cuboids are cubes

#### Example Activities
- "Given rectangles of dimensions 3×3, 4×4, 2×5, 7×2, etc. — which cuboids can be built? Are any cubes?"
- "Build the structure shown in the diagram using real cubes"
- "Show various nets of the same cuboid, and some non-nets"

## Topic ז — סימטרייה (Symmetry) — 4 hours

### Types of Symmetry

#### סימטרייה שיקופית (Reflective / Line Symmetry)
- A shape has reflective symmetry if it can be mapped onto itself by reflection
- **קו הסימטרייה** (line of symmetry / axis of symmetry): the reflection line
- Investigation of which shapes have lines of symmetry (including squares and rectangles)

#### סימטרייה סיבובית (Rotational Symmetry)
- **מרכז הסימטרייה** (center of symmetry): a point about which rotation by less than 360° maps the shape onto itself

#### Application to Triangles
- Classify triangles by their symmetry properties
- Equilateral triangle: 3 lines of symmetry + rotational symmetry of order 3
- Isosceles triangle: 1 line of symmetry
- Scalene triangle: no symmetry

#### Skills
- Identify shapes with line symmetry
- Draw lines of symmetry
- Identify shapes with rotational symmetry
- Classify triangles by symmetry

## Topic ח — מדידות (Measurements) — 11 hours

### Subtopic ח.1 — שטח והיקף מלבן (Area and Perimeter of a Rectangle) — 5 hours

#### Units
- **Area units**: סמ"ר (cm²), מ"ר (m²)
- **Length units** (review): מ"מ (mm), מ"ס (cm), מ (m), מ"ק (km)

#### Area Formula
- Formula for rectangle area is based on the **meaning of area as the number of unit squares covering the rectangle**
- A = length × width
- Not presented as a rote formula — built from understanding

#### Skills
- Calculate area and perimeter of rectangles
- Calculate area of composite shapes made of rectangles
- Perform activities involving areas and perimeters **without formulas** (intuitive/exploratory)
- Estimate length and area
- Find maximum area for a given perimeter (exploratory: "given a fixed perimeter, find the rectangle with maximum area")
- Calculate the amount of paint needed to paint a room

#### Example Question Types — Area and Perimeter
- "Calculate the amount of paint needed to paint a room of dimensions 4 × 5 × 3 meters"
- "Given a fixed perimeter, find the rectangle with the maximum area"
- "Calculate the area of a shape composed of two rectangles"
- Estimation activities for length and area

### Subtopic ח.2 — נפח תיבה ושטח פנים (Volume of a Cuboid and Surface Area) — 4 hours

#### Units
- **Volume units**:
  - מ"ל (milliliter) = סמ"ק (cubic centimeter / cc)
  - ליטר (liter) = דצ"מ מעוקב (cubic decimeter)
  - מ"ק (cubic meter)

#### Volume Concept
- Volume is measured by **filling with unit cubes** (1cm³ cubes)
- This leads to the conclusion that volume of a cuboid = length × width × height

#### Skills
- Calculate volume of a cuboid
- Calculate surface area of a cuboid (as practice for rectangle area and for 3D spatial reasoning)
- Understand how changing side lengths changes volume and surface area
- Understand specific gravity (משקל סגולי) and relate volume to weight for different materials

#### Example Activities
- "Build a cuboid from 24 cubes. How many different ways can this be done?"
- "Given a cuboid, design three new cuboids with volumes that are:
  - 8 times larger
  - 4 times larger
  - 2 times larger"
- "Estimate the volume of a cuboid"
- Weight-volume connection: calculate weight of cuboids made of different materials

## Specific: Multiplication Table Coverage

The curriculum document does not present a separate multiplication table topic in grade 4 — multiplication table facts are assumed mastered from grade 3. In grade 4:

- **Mental multiplication by whole tens and hundreds** is explicitly taught (e.g., 30×20, 400×5)
- Students apply multiplication facts within the **long division** algorithm
- The estimation game "4 in a Row" (page 9) uses factor pairs: 12, 13, 14, 19, 20, 24, 26, 28 — suggesting products in the range of small 2-digit multiplications (12–28 × other small numbers)
- Grade 4 extends multiplication to **2-digit × 2-digit** using the distributive law
- Students build intuition about multiplication through number sense tasks (e.g., "if you know 14×5=70, then 14×50=?")

## General Pedagogical Notes

1. **Concrete before abstract**: All new concepts (fractions, volume, area) must first be experienced with physical objects and visual models before moving to symbols.

2. **Multiple representations**: Students must encounter each concept through at least 2–3 different models (e.g., fractions via circles, rectangles, number lines).

3. **Discovery over rules**: Divisibility rules, equivalent fractions, and volume formulas are all derived by students through investigation — not handed to them as memorized rules.

4. **Estimation is fundamental**: Estimation is not a peripheral skill — it is integral to every arithmetic topic and must be woven into all practice.

5. **Language precision matters**: The curriculum explicitly corrects common language errors in long division. Question generation should use mathematically precise language.

6. **No premature algorithms**: Students must not use the algorithm for fraction reducing/expanding in Grade 4. Fraction operations are based on models and intuition only.

7. **Connections between operations**: The distributive law (חוק הפילוג) underlies both multi-digit multiplication and long division. Questions should help students see these connections.

8. **Calculator use**: Operations with numbers larger than 1,000 may be performed with a calculator. The curriculum explicitly states this.

9. **Cross-topic integration**: The 12 hours of "comprehensive questions" (ד.5) are explicitly meant to integrate all arithmetic operations together in word problem contexts, including measurement contexts.

10. **Advanced vs. standard**: The curriculum distinguishes between tasks for all students and tasks for "advanced classes" (כיתות מתקדמות). Fraction-as-quotient, algebraic notation of properties, and 3-digit-factor multiplication are flagged as advanced.

11. **Hebrew calendar**: Gematria and Hebrew calendar calculations are integral curriculum content, not enrichment.

12. **Parity (זוגיות)**: Students should determine whether a result is odd or even without computing, through number structure reasoning.
